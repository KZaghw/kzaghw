I am an MSc student in Computational Neuroscience at the University of Tübingen. I am interested in how learning agents construct useful representations from raw experience: how they discover concepts and causal structure through interaction, choose informative actions, and revise their understanding when it proves insufficient.

During my master’s, I have worked on Bayesian distributional reinforcement learning, hierarchical generative models, and causal abstraction. These projects have led me toward questions in causal representation learning, abstraction, and decision-making under uncertainty that I would like to pursue in a PhD.

## Current and Recent Research

**Student research assistant (HiWi), Max Planck Institute for Biological Cybernetics.** I am currently working on Bayesian approaches to reinforcement learning and Monte Carlo tree search, with an emphasis on representing epistemic uncertainty and using it during exploration and planning.

**Lab rotation, Robust Machine Learning Group, ELLIS Institute Tübingen.** I studied when block-identifiable learned representations preserve high-level causal abstractions under changes of representation. The resulting manuscript is available as a public preprint and is currently under review.
